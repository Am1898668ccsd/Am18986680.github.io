01 / The case

A free ride-planning app

RouteBuddy gives useful turn-by-turn directions. It asks for location access all the time, even when you are not using it. Its privacy notice says location may be shared with “partners” to improve services, but does not name them.

⌖

What could be captured?

GPS points over time can reveal a route, approximate home and school/work, regular visits, and daily routines—not just the destination you typed.

↗

Why is that a concern?

Unexpected collection or sharing can expose sensitive routines and remove your control. A useful feature does not automatically justify collecting location in the background.

Keep these questions separate: ethics asks what is responsible and respectful; law asks what rules apply. An action can be legal yet still raise an ethical concern.

04 / Law is not the whole answer

What COPPA does—and does not—say

Jurisdiction: United States (federal). The Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act Rule (COPPA), 16 C.F.R. Part 312, covers operators of child-directed websites or online services that collect personal information from children under 13, and general-audience services with actual knowledge they are collecting it from a child under 13. The Rule includes notice and verifiable parental-consent requirements, with exceptions. The FTC Rule treats persistent identifiers and geolocation data sufficient to identify a street and city as personal information.

COPPA is not a blanket privacy law for every person or every app. Whether it applies depends on the service, the user, and the data collection. For older users, or when COPPA does not apply, data minimization and clear permission choices can still be responsible ethical practices.

This is an educational summary, not legal advice. Check the current rule and the app’s actual practices.
